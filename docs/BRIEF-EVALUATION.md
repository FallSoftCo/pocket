# NextComp briefing evaluation

The selected [full briefing](NEXTCOMP-BRIEF.md) explains personal agency, mechanisms and versioned runtime evidence. Seven drafts and nine qualitative evaluations helped identify what readers understood and where they asked for stronger proof. These were generic ChatGPT/Codex reviewers, not Tibo, his agent or actual Dots. The exercise measures reconstruction and objections; it cannot predict whether he will engage.

## Descriptions tested

| Draft | Emphasis | What it contributed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Outcome first | A compact statement of directing ongoing work from Android. |
| B | Lived workflow | A concrete illustrative sequence across coordinator, sessions and alerts. |
| C | Mechanism and research | Runtime/control architecture and questions for a personal-agent system. |
| D | Full capability and evidence | Enough scope to reconstruct non-coding applications and the interaction surfaces. |
| E | Person and agency | What the person can accomplish, rather than starting with a stack or feature inventory. |
| F | Agency with inspectable mechanisms | Source-backed coordination/delivery details and corrected native-audio account authentication. |
| G | Agency and versioned live evidence | Adds existing physical-phone and clean-environment execution records while keeping their limits explicit. |

G is recommended because it lets a reviewer reconstruct the broad purpose and inspect concrete paths from instruction to work and back. Shorter descriptions made it too easy to reduce the system to a remote coding interface. A long feature list alone also failed to answer the more useful question: why would this improve a person's actual continuity and attention?

## What was actually evaluated

Two fresh anonymous ChatGPT conversations evaluated A and B using guided questions. Seven ephemeral stock Codex CLI conversations evaluated A, C, D, E, F, a D source-corroboration pass, and G with versioned evidence. No separately billed API-key evaluation service was used. Prompts varied between guided reconstruction, neutral reconstruction and source-backed inspection. This was not a controlled ranking, and reviewers were not modeled as a known individual.

The first evaluations were asked to reconstruct supported actions and applications, separate implemented claims from examples, and identify reasons to continue or dismiss. Later neutral evaluations avoided suggesting non-coding examples. Source-backed passes inspected implementation and qualification records. The D source-corroboration pass read the whole draft bundle and earlier responses; it is explicitly non-blind. G's evidence review received only its selected draft and relevant source/verification material. Its verdict recognized concrete execution paths beyond a feature list, while still requesting a current demonstration and reliability evidence.

The shared browser was signed out of ChatGPT. Two anonymous evaluations completed; a later submission required login. No sign-in was attempted and no actual Dot was accessed. No recipient channel has been verified, and nothing was sent to Tibo or another external recipient.

## Findings and corrections

Reviewers recovered general computing through the user's existing environments, coordinator/direct-session control, intermediate interaction and phone-local execution. They repeatedly asked for a real workflow, installation and recovery evidence. That is a request to demonstrate usefulness, not evidence that the product's intended scope is only coding.

Source inspection corrected two important claims. Compatible native voice uses stock Codex transport with the host's ChatGPT authentication; it does not require a separate audio API key. The coordinator's brief read-only role is enforced in review mode, but is instructional under full permissions. Source review also identified incomplete runtime qualification around delayed transcript completion, text-turn identity conflicts, unknown-outcome reconciliation and interrupted native playback. These remain follow-up areas, not solved claims in the selected brief.

Editorial review removed an unsupported comparison implying that hosted personal agents cannot use the user's own computer. Official Dots documentation explicitly supports connected computers and local tasks. Existing remote products also offer voice, notifications and approvals. The selected brief therefore asks readers to inspect NextComp's particular interaction and execution behavior rather than treating its ingredient list as unique.

## How to use the selected briefing

Send the [full briefing](NEXTCOMP-BRIEF.md) as the primary artifact, with the [capability map](CAPABILITIES.md) and [versioned verification](VERIFICATION.md) available for follow-up. Keep the complete explanation accessible to recipient agents; a short introduction can point to it, but should not replace it. Use a current live example with intermediate intervention and reconnection as the next proof. Do not describe an illustrative multi-session sequence as already qualified end to end.

The public briefing has been edited for current release status and supported comparisons after its evaluation. Private task transcripts, recipient guesses, evaluator workstation logs and deployment configuration are intentionally excluded. A future recipient-specific evaluation should use the actual supported channel and preserve its feedback separately from this generic comprehension exercise.
