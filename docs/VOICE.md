# Voice alpha 8

Pocket uses stock Codex app-server's experimental native WebRTC voice transport and the host's ChatGPT authentication. A separate audio API key is not required. The session list opens the persistent coordinator; a conversation's microphone opens voice with that existing session selected. The coordinator retains context, uses real Pocket control tools, and can return to global coordination with select({coordinator:true}). No prescribed command phrases are required.

The bottom Talk control opens the coordinator and starts recording immediately. Session-card Talk controls open voice with that session selected. One tap on the chat microphone control stops recording and automatically sends the completed turn; volume down has the same start/stop behavior. Nonblocking microphone reads make stop independent of another hardware press. The coordinator is a regular chat view with equal-width 64dp microphone and keyboard controls at the bottom, secondary text entry behind a keyboard button (Enter sends), replay/pause and an on-screen media-volume slider. Saved transcripts and replies load from device-scoped backend history with pagination, including after app restarts. Manual recording remains local until stop. Completed recordings are delivered to an isolated audio-only native thread. Partial native transcript segments are combined, allowed to settle, and committed once to the durable coordinator. The audio-only thread has a read-only sandbox and any backend handoff is interrupted; it cannot use partial transcripts to perform task work. The coordinator uses the complete transcript and current session focus. Speech is rendered through native voice, buffered into a complete recording, and played with pause/resume/replay. Ending voice does not interrupt work sessions.

Turn IDs and controller/tool results are persistent. Pending recordings survive app process death. After re-entering voice, Pocket checks the existing turn before delivering audio again. Disconnects, server restarts and uncertain tool delivery are reported for inspection rather than automatically replayed. Completed IDs remain pending until response playback begins, so speech preparation failures can replay the saved result without re-running actions.

First-launch prerequisites: paired backend, stock Codex with native voice and ChatGPT sign-in, microphone permission. Accessibility is optional in the foreground and needed for global volume buttons. Android may disable it after updates. A user-visible microphone/media foreground service owns recording and playback. Recordings are 16 kHz mono WAV, capped at 120 seconds; silence is discarded. A lost audio-focus event cancels an unfinished recording or pauses speech. Pocket disconnects native audio after 30 seconds ready and while locally recording, reconnecting on delivery; the persistent coordinator and cached replay remain available. This avoids paying for an idle audio stream. The transport also has a 90-second client lease, renewed every 30 seconds; abandoned connections stop billing after expiry. Stopping voice sends an explicit native stop.

Validation: isolated stock CLI tests without OPENAI_API_KEY or CODEX_API_KEY established connected WebRTC, transcribed a complete recorded request across multiple native segments, rendered a supplied response and completed buffered playback. Server regression tests cover latest-message selection, bounded preview hydration, audio-thread isolation, owner checks, combined transcripts and idempotent commit. Android unit tests/build/lint and emulator checks are recorded during release qualification. Physical Pixel lock-screen/background key behavior and Bluetooth routing require device checks; emulator qualification does not establish those hardware behaviors.

Limitations: native voice is experimental and depends on account/CLI compatibility and network access. Rendering and buffering full responses trades some startup latency for reliable manual pause/replay. Audio arrives at native voice in real time after recording finishes. Transcription settlement is bounded; disconnected or missing transcripts are retried without executing partial work. Native desktop credit pricing is documented, but the exact promotional-grant debit through CLI transport has not been measured. No silent fallback to separately billed API audio occurs.

Generic error WAV assets were authored using Mini TTS, Marin, on October 2, 2026; they contain no user/task data. The older API audio adapter is retained for compatibility but is not used by the new default voice flow.

The conversation and coordinator share a bottom action dock. Text input appears only after selecting Keyboard, with Enter still sending. Session options open in a bottom sheet; active work keeps Stop directly accessible. Follow, queue access, return-to-sessions and speech replay are also reachable at the bottom. The session list uses bottom Find / New controls for search, filters, creation and profile switching. Weekly allowance is compact and noninteractive.

Session age colors use a fixed continuous scale: linear across the first day, then logarithmic through 90 days, with full-color session cards and timestamps. Color is an additional cue, not a replacement for dates.

UX reference review used Mobbin’s [Urban Company action sheet](https://mobbin.com/screens/e8cd488e-3da3-4b3a-9a89-e478c79c9271) and [BFF chat controls](https://mobbin.com/screens/db352f6d-4d7d-41f1-a678-e66ee043da83), inspected as screen images. Built for Mars MCP recommended bottom docks, contextual sheets and clear feedback; its retrieved [button-feedback example](https://builtformars.com/ux-bites/the-button-effect) supports the feedback principle. These are design references, not usability-test evidence for Pocket.

NeXT palette: black/white and neutral gray surfaces; yellow #FFC600, green #00A85D, vermilion #FF675D and cerise #EB4D97 match the RGB fills in the [logo SVG on Paul Rand’s official site](https://assets.paulrand.design/Works/NeXT/NeXT-Computers-1986.svg). These are source-matched digital RGB values, not a claim about original print ink/Pantone equivalence. Recency interpolates these anchors continuously; dark charcoal cards use strong colored recency rails and black-on-color timestamp badges.

Back and active Stop use large 64dp bottom targets. Opening text input hides the mic/keyboard selector. A conversation-level overlay-priority Android back callback takes precedence over IME dismissal, returning to the session list even while the keyboard is open; voice exit also clears the selected conversation. Secondary sheets retain their own dismissal behavior.

The contemporary NeXT treatment uses a consistent 16dp grid, compact 6dp control/card corners, high-contrast black/charcoal surfaces, source-matched vivid accents and simple sans-serif typography. Session cards prioritize title, recent message and last activity, omit the recency legend and remove redundant idle/project subtitles. Tap targets remain 64dp for primary controls and 48dp for card actions.

### NextComp interaction refinement (alpha 9)

The Android display name is NextComp; package IDs, deep links and the existing pairing remain compatible. The original NC monogram uses the same NeXT palette. The list no longer duplicates the coordinator in a pinned card: bottom Talk opens it and retains its history. Each session card offers direct Talk and Keyboard entry. The single-line header shows the icon, connection indicator, colored allowance and reset deadline, without an app-name heading or color legend.

A local, per-profile usage history estimates depletion only after at least 30 minutes and one percentage point of observed consumption. Estimates are suppressed when stale/disconnected, reset windows change, allowance increases, or projected depletion occurs after the reset. The estimate is approximate and based on the observed rate, not a guarantee; it is displayed alongside the reset time.

The session list sorts by last interaction and lays out from bottom to top: the newest session rests directly above the bottom dock, and scrolling upward reveals older sessions. Recency remains an absolute linear/logarithmic color scale on a 10dp rail and solid timestamp badge, without large bright card surfaces.

Dark typography refinement uses bundled Inter by Rasmus Andersson (SIL Open Font License; license included), regular body text and medium-weight controls. The [ChatGPT dark project chat list on Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/screens/6ff3b745-b7b6-479e-94fa-16558a5c7839) informed the black surfaces, lighter hierarchy and compact text treatment. The list refreshes while visible and live response deltas update its previews; working sessions show a progress indicator.

### Compact glowing cards (alpha 10)

Cards use a radial recency-colored glow over charcoal rather than a left rail. A compact timestamp and animated working indicator share the title row; latest text and Talk/Keyboard are the remaining two rows. Actual preview, status or activity changes illuminate a yellow outline that fades after 2.5 seconds, with no slide animation or initial-load flash. The bottom-first ordering is retained.

Alpha 10 replaces the initial NC monogram with an original faceted N mark, carrying the NeXT palette in green/yellow stems and cerise/vermilion diagonal accents. Launcher, header, notification silhouette and repository SVG share the same geometry.
